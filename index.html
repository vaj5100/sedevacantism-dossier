Argument skeleton §

Major (from Part 1–3 videos): The Church is infallible in (a) her solemn and ordinary universal teaching,
  (b) her universal disciplinary laws, (c) her universally promulgated sacramental rites.
  A true pope cannot bind the universal Church to error, harmful law, or an invalid/doubtful rite.

Minor:
  I.  Dignitatis Humanae teaches as revealed doctrine what Quanta Cura solemnly condemned.
  II. Vatican II / post-conciliar teaching and the 1983 Code reverse the doctrine of the one Church
      and laws which canonists held to apply divine law (c. 1258, c. 731 §2).
  III. Paul VI promulgated an episcopal form which fails Pius XII's univocity test; at minimum the
      resulting consecrations are positively doubtful, and doubtful sacraments may not be used (DZ 1151).

Conclusion: The authority that promulgated these cannot be the authority Christ guarantees;
  therefore the promulgators did not hold that authority (sede vacante).

Thesis structure note: I and II are doctrinal (they prove “not a pope” via infallibility + heresy). III is sacramental and works differently: it does not by itself prove vacancy (an invalid rite could in theory be promulgated by a non-pope OR a valid one only if you deny the Church’s indefectibility in rites), but it (a) reinforces the infallibility argument (Cekada §X.B) and (b) has a practical consequence: post-1968 “bishops” — including Ratzinger, consecrated 28 May 1977 — are doubtful, so even a future “conservative” claimant elected from them could be doubtfully a bishop.

Vatican II — Dignitatis Humanae (vatican.va, verified) §

  • DH 2: “no one is to be forced to act in a manner contrary to his own beliefs, whether privately or publicly… the right to religious freedom has its foundation in the very dignity of the human person as this dignity is known through the revealed word of God and by reason itself.   • DH 2: “the right to this immunity continues to exist even in those who do not live up to their obligation of seeking the truth and adhering to it”
  • DH 6: even where “special civil recognition is given to one religious community… it is at the same time imperative that the right of all citizens and religious communities to religious freedom should be recognized and made effective in practice.”
  • DH 7: limit is “public order”.
  • DH 9: “Revelation does not indeed affirm in so many words the right of man to immunity from external coercion in matters religious.”
  • Footnotes cite Libertas, Immortale Dei, Ci Riesce — never Quanta Cura or the Syllabus (checked).

The contradiction, point by point §

  1. QC condemns a liberty of worship that is a “personal right… [which] ought to be legally proclaimed”; DH 2 says the right “is to become a civil right” in constitutional law. Same structure.
  2. QC condemns limiting repression only by “public peace”; Leo’s condemned theory uses “public order” (ID §25); DH 7 adopts “public order”.
  3. Old doctrine: tolerance is a concession of prudence; DH: an inviolable natural right grounded in nature and revelation.
  4. Syllabus 77–78 condemned dropping the confessional state; DH 6 requires religious freedom even in a confessional state. Real-world application: Spain’s 1967 organic law changed Article 6 of the Spanish Charter explicitly citing DH 2 (Pivarunas 1995).

  • John Courtney Murray, SJ (DH drafter), Georgetown Woodstock archive 1966c: the old doctrine “can hardly be regarded as permanent Catholic doctrine”; DH means “discarding an older theory of civil tolerance in favor of a new doctrine of religious freedom”.
  • Lefebvre, letter 3 Sept 1975 (fsspx.org): “compare it with … Mirari vos … or with Quanta cura … and you can recognize the contradiction almost word for word.”
  • Fr. Brian Harrison (catholicculture.org recnum=9020) concedes: if DH affirmed “a natural right not to be prevented from publicly propagating non-Catholic religions in Catholic societies, then indeed the Declaration would implicitly contradict” prior teaching. → DH 2 (“in his very nature”, “publicly”) + DH 6 (confessional states) meets his own condition.

Counter-arguments you must answer §

  1. Immunity, not a right to error (DH 1; conciliar relatio: spreading error is “abusing” the right). Reply: QC condemns exactly an immunity guaranteed by law; a juridical right to immunity for public cult is what was condemned.
  2. QC condemned only unlimited/naturalist liberty (Harrison 1987; Fox). Reply: QC §3 condemns the right itself, and the “public peace” limitation — DH’s limit is the same kind.
  3. Mere public-law/disciplinary change (Harrison: like Friday abstinence). Reply: Leo frames it as reason/natural law (“contrary to reason”); DH claims grounds in revelation — neither is a policy register.
  4. Benedict: principles vs contingent decisions. Reply: §6 is a solemn act of apostolic authority; contingency claim is unproven.
  5. Ci Riesce itself admits God may not give right to repress. Reply: “no mandate to repress” ≠ positive natural right of the erring.
  6. Pink: State lacks competence; Church’s coercive authority untouched (First Things 2012).

The contradictions §

  1. Identity vs. subsistence: HG 27 binds Catholics to “one and the same thing”; LG 8 / UUS 11 / DI 17 place the Church of Christ “present”, “operative” in non-Catholic bodies. Ratzinger himself (2000, catholicculture.org recnum=3920): “this difference between subsistit and est cannot be fully resolved from the logical viewpoint.”
  2. Membership: MC 22 — divided in faith = not members; UR 3 — “members of Christ’s body”.
  3. Means of salvation: MA 10 — no communion with Christ the Head outside; UR 3 — separated communities used by the Spirit as means of salvation.
  4. Worship: MA 8 / c. 1258 / c. 2316 vs UR 8, Assisi 1986, JPII 1998 audience.
  5. Sacraments to non-Catholics: c. 731 §2 “even those erring in good faith” vs c. 844 §§3–4 + Directory §159 — this is universal law, which ties into the speaker’s secondary-object-of-infallibility argument (Van Noort).
  6. Pius XI’s condemned premise (religions “more or less good”) ↔ NA 2 + 1998 audience (religions “arose” from openness inspired by the Spirit).

Key reply to the “it’s just discipline” objection: John Daly (ed. John Lane), The Principal Heresies and Other Errors of Vatican II (isidore.co PDF): c. 1258 “represents in part an application of the Divine Law; and not even a pope can abolish a Divine law.” (Daly’s PDF misattributes MA to Pius IX — don’t copy that error. His cited Cardinal Allen 1592 / Clement VIII ruling is Unverified.)

Counter-arguments §

  1. CDF Responses (2007) Q1/Q3: Council “neither changed nor intended to change this doctrine”; “subsists” “indicates the full identity”. Footnote 4 quotes the Council’s own commission: “solam Ecclesiam catholicam esse veram Ecclesiam Christi.” — Strongest counter. Reply: authors’ intent vs. the plain text and subsequent universal practice (UUS 11, DI 17, c. 844); a Commentary declaring “no discontinuity” doesn’t remove the textual one.
  2. Harrison, “Is Ecumenism a Heresy?” (2009): concedes a disciplinary “U-turn”, denies doctrinal contradiction. Reply: divine-law basis of c. 1258 (Daly); UUS 11 / DI 17 assert a federation-like presence of the Church.
  3. 1949 Instruction allowed some mixed meetings and the Our Father with permission. Reply: still forbade communicatio in sacris and required “return”.
  4. Conciliar qualifiers: UR 3 “fully”; LG 14 necessity of the Church; DI 21–22 other religions “gravely deficient”. Reply: qualifiers don’t cancel affirmations; contradiction stands where both are asserted.
  5. MC §103 (“unconscious desire… a certain relationship”) as precedent. Reply: individuals’ desire ≠ communities as means of salvation.

III. Doubtfulness of the 1968 Episcopal Consecration Rite §

The two forms §

  • Pius XII, Sacramentum Ordinis §4–5 (1947), AAS 40 (1948) 5–7 (vatican.va Latin):
    • Rule: the form is the words “quibus univoce significantur effectus sacramentales, — scilicet potestas Ordinis et gratia Spiritus Sancti”.
    • §1: “Ecclesiae nulla competat potestas in substantiam Sacramentorum”.
    • Old form: «Comple in Sacerdote tuo ministerii tui summam, et ornamentis totius glorificationis instructum coelestis unguenti rore sanctifica.»
  • Paul VI, Pontificalis Romani (18 June 1968) (vatican.va Latin):
    • Repeats Pius XII’s univocity rule verbatim.
    • Claims the prayer comes from the “Traditio Apostolica Hippolyti Romani… quaeque, magna ex parte, in liturgia Ordinationis Coptorum et Syrorum occidentalium adhuc servatur.”
    • New form: «Et nunc effunde super hunc Electum eam virtutem, quae a te est, Spiritum principalem, quem dedisti dilecto Filio Tuo Iesu Christo, quem Ipse donavit sanctis Apostolis, qui constituerunt Ecclesiam per singula loca, ut sanctuarium tuum, in gloriam et laudem indeficientem nominis tui.»
    • No summa ministerii, no sacerdos, no summum sacerdotium in the essential words (that phrase appears only in the preamble/context).

Governing principles §

  • Leo XIII, Apostolicae Curae (1896) (newadvent.org/library/docs_le13ac.htm):
    • §24: sacraments “ought both to signify the grace which they effect”; the imposition of hands “by itself signifies nothing definite” → the form must specify the Order.
    • §25: “‘Receive the Holy Ghost,’ certainly do not in the least definitely express the sacred Order of Priesthood… or its grace and power” — closest analogue to “Spiritum principalem”.
    • §27: deliberate removal of what “sets forth the dignity and office of the priesthood” makes the form insufficient.
    • §33: defect of intention when a rite is changed to introduce “another rite not approved by the Church”. (caveat: opponents say Paul VI’s rite was approved — so this argument only works after you’ve established I/II)
  • Innocent XI / Holy Office, 4 March 1679, condemned proposition 1 (DZ 1151; modern DH 2101 — not checked): condemned: “It is not illicit in conferring sacraments to follow a probable opinion regarding the value of the sacrament, the safer opinion being abandoned… Therefore, one should not make use of probable opinions only in conferring baptism, sacerdotal or episcopal orders.” (romanmissal.org/denzinger/p357.html) → Tutiorism is binding. Even the SSPX concedes it: Fr. Peter Scott, “it is forbidden to accept a likely or probably valid ordination for the subsequent conferring of sacraments.” (sspx.org/en/must-priests-who-come-tradition-be-re-ordained-30479)

IV. Synthesis — Why the Novus Ordo Is a New Religion §

This section ties I–III together. It does not add a fourth contradiction; it argues that the three changes, taken together with the new liturgy, amount to a different religion rather than a reformed one.

1. The framework: Bellarmine’s three bonds §

St. Robert Bellarmine, De Ecclesia Militante, Lib. III, cap. 2 (Venice 1721 ed., Tomus II, p. 53; scan: archive.org/details/bub_gb_SfJq79_HdscC). Latin normalized from OCR:

…illam unam & veram esse coetum hominum ejusdem Christianae fidei professione, & eorundem Sacramentorum communione colligatum, sub regimine legitimorum pastorum, ac praecipue unius Christi in terris Vicarii Romani Pontificis. Tres enim sunt partes hujus definitionis. Professio verae fidei, Sacramentorum communio, & subjectio ad legitimum pastorem Romanum Pontificem.

English (Hardon, hardonsj.org/christ-to-catholicism-definition-catholic-church/): “the one and true Church is the assembly of men, bound together by the profession of the same Christian faith, and by the communion of the same sacraments, under the rule of legitimate pastors, and in particular of the one Vicar of Christ on earth, the Roman Pontiff.”

Bellarmine on the first bond excludes not only Jews, Turks and pagans but those “qui fuerunt, & recesserunt, ut haeretici & apostatae” (who were in it and receded from it).

Rome itself uses the same triad. Francis, Letter to Bishops with Traditionis Custodes (16 July 2021, vatican.va): Vatican II “reaffirmed the external bonds of incorporation in the Church — the profession of faith, the sacraments, of communion”.

Thesis claim: a religion is identified by (1) what it professes, (2) how it worships and sanctifies, (3) whom it obeys. The post-conciliar body changed all three. Same buildings, same titles — different religion.

2. The bridge principle: worship professes faith §

  • Pius XII, Mediator Dei §47 (vatican.va): “The worship she offers to God… is a continuous profession of Catholic faith… The entire liturgy, therefore, has the Catholic faith for its content, inasmuch as it bears public witness to the faith of the Church.”
  • Mediator Dei §48: “let the rule of belief determine the rule of prayer” (Lex credendi legem statuat supplicandi).
    • Do not cite §46 in your favour: there Pius XII rejects using the liturgy as a “proving ground” of doctrine. Use §47–48: worship is a profession of faith, so a changed worship is a changed profession → Bellarmine’s first bond.
  • Leo XIII, Apostolicae Curae §30 — the precedent that makes this a “new religion” argument, not just a “bad liturgy” argument. Of Cranmer’s reformers: “Being fully cognisant of the necessary connection between faith and worship, between ‘the law of believing and the law of praying’, under a pretext of returning to the primitive form, they corrupted the Liturgical Order in many ways to suit the errors of the reformers.” (newadvent.org/library/docs_le13ac.htm)
    • Anglicanism is the textbook case of a new religion produced by rewriting the rites while keeping bishops, cathedrals and vestments. Your thesis argues 1965–1972 is the same operation.
  • Lefebvre, Declaration of 21 Nov 1974 (fsspx.org/en/1974-declaration-archbishop-lefebvre-31164): “It is impossible to modify profoundly the lex orandi without modifying the lex credendi. Corresponding with a new mass we have a new catechism, a new priesthood, new seminaries…”

3. Bond one — a new profession of faith §

The standard: Vatican I, Dei Filius ch. 4 (vatican.va Latin): “sacrorum quoque dogmatum is sensus perpetuo est retinendus, quem semel declaravit Sancta Mater Ecclesia, nec umquam ab eo sensu, altior intelligentiae specie et nomine, recedendum.” Canon IV.3 anathematizes anyone who says a sense may be given to dogmas “alius ab eo, quem intellexit et intelligit Ecclesia”.

  • Tanner English: “there must never be any abandonment of this sense under the pretext or in the name of a more profound understanding.”

Sworn by every cleric 1910–1967: Oath against Modernism (Pius X, Sacrorum Antistitum, AAS 2 (1910)): “I entirely reject the heretical misrepresentation that dogmas evolve and change from one meaning to another different from the one which the Church held previously.” (Latin haereticum commentum = heretical fabrication.)

D. The post-conciliar contradiction: the sign of unity given to those outside it §

Rome still teaches the Eucharist presupposes full unity:

  • Ecclesia de Eucharistia §35: “The celebration of the Eucharist… cannot be the starting-point for communion; it presupposes that communion already exists.”
  • §38: “It is not possible to give communion to a person who… rejects the full truth of the faith regarding the Eucharistic mystery… the sacrament of his body and blood does not permit duplicity.”
  • §44: the Church’s unity “absolutely requires full communion in the bonds of the profession of faith, the sacraments and ecclesiastical governance.”
  • §30: Catholics must not receive at Protestant services “so as not to condone an ambiguity about the nature of the Eucharist and… to bear clear witness to the truth.”
  • 1993 Ecumenical Directory §129: “Eucharistic communion is inseparably linked to full ecclesial communion and its visible expression.” §12: the Church is “united in the three-fold bond of faith, sacramental life and hierarchical ministry.”
  • Unitatis Redintegratio §2: the Eucharist is that “by which the unity of His Church is both signified and made a reality.”

Yet the same authority gives the sign to those who lack the unity:

  • 1983 CIC c. 844 §3: Eucharist licitly to Eastern non-Catholics “if they seek such on their own accord and are properly disposed” — no profession of faith required at all.
  • c. 844 §4: to Protestants in grave necessity if they “manifest Catholic faith in respect to these sacraments” — partial, not integral, faith.
  • Ecclesia de Eucharistia §45: admitted “to meet a grave spiritual need… not to bring about an intercommunion.” §46: Catholics too may request sacraments from non-Catholic ministers.
  • Unitatis Redintegratio §8 (Latin): “Significatio unitatis plerumque vetat communicationem. Gratia procuranda quandoque illam commendat.” — signifying unity for the most part forbids common worship; grace sometimes commends it. §15: communicatio in sacris with the Orthodox “is not only possible but to be encouraged.”

Against the pre-conciliar law and teaching:

  • 1917 c. 731 §2: sacraments forbidden to heretics/schismatics “etiam bona fide errantibus” unless reconciled.
  • 1917 c. 1258: active participation in non-Catholic worship forbidden “quovis modo”.
  • Ex Quo §§22–23: a schismatic patriarch could not even be named in the Canon to be prayed for.
  • Thomas, q.82 a.9: “it is not lawful to receive Communion from them, or to assist at their mass.”

The point: Before 1958 the Church would not let a schismatic’s name be read at the altar. After the Council she lets him receive Communion — the very sign of the unity he lacks — while her own documents say the sacrament “does not permit duplicity” (EdE §38). Either the Eucharist signifies full unity of faith (then c. 844 §3 falsifies the sign) or it doesn’t (then Ecclesia de Eucharistia, Trent and Thomas are wrong). This is pillar II (ecumenism) made sacramental, and bond S broken from Rome’s side.

VI. The Visibility of the Church — The Main Objection, and the Answers §

This is the objection the thesis stands or falls on. Stated by Abp. Lefebvre in 1979: “The visibility of the Church is too necessary to its existence for it to be possible that God would allow that visibility to disappear for decades.” The section works in five steps: A what the doctrine actually requires; B the objection at full strength; C the answers, layer by layer; D what the thesis must concede; E the recommended position.

Bond key as before: F faith · S sacraments · P pastors.

A. What the doctrine requires — and what it does not §

The Church is necessarily visible.

  • Leo XIII, Satis Cognitum §3 (1896) (vatican.va): “in regard to those who constitute it, and to the things which lead to these spiritual gifts, it is external and necessarily visible.” … “those who arbitrarily conjure up and picture to themselves a hidden and invisible Church are in grievous and pernicious error.” … “since the Church is such by divine will and constitution, such it must uniformly remain to the end of time.”
  • Satis Cognitum §10: the Church is “one in faith, one in end, one in the participation of the means adapted to the attainment of the end, and one as subject to one and the same authority.” — Leo’s own form of the three bonds. FSP
  • Pius XII, Mystici Corporis §14 (vatican.va): the Church is “something definite and perceptible to the senses… they err in a matter of divine truth, who imagine the Church to be invisible.” §65: “There can, then, be no real opposition or conflict between the invisible mission of the Holy spirit and the juridical commission of Ruler and Teacher received from Christ.” P
  • → No “invisible Church” answer is available to the thesis. The reply must locate a visible Church.

How visibility is defined — the three bonds again.

  • Van Noort, Christ’s Church (archive.org/details/vannoortvol2christschurch), p. 12: the members “are bound together by external bonds so as to form a society that can be perceived by the senses, a society of such a nature that one can readily discern who belongs to it and who does not.” p. 13: “It is by these bonds that the Church is drawn into unity and held together; without them there is simply no Church of Christ.” FSP
  • Catholic Encyclopedia, “The Church” (Joyce 1908, newadvent.org/cathen/03744a.htm): two kinds of visibility, “‘material’ and ‘formal’… The material visibility of the Church involves no more than that it must ever be a public, not a private profession; a society manifest to the world, not a body whose members are bound by some secret tie.” “Formal visibility is secured by those attributes which are usually termed the ‘notes’ of the Church.”
  • Same article: “The power to recognize the Church for what it is presupposes certain moral dispositions… the incapacity to see is due, not to the want of visibility in the Church, but to the blindness of the individual.”
  • Berry, The Church of Christ (1927), p. 70 (archive.org/details/churchofchristap00berr): visibility “does not mean that all will actually recognize it as such.”

Indefectibility is promised to the whole, and to the See of Rome — not to every part.

  • CE, “The Church”: the Church “can never become corrupt in faith or in morals; nor can it ever lose the Apostolic hierarchy, or the sacraments.” But: “The gift of indefectibility plainly does not guarantee each several part of the Church against heresy or apostasy… Only to One particular Church is indefectibility assured, viz. to the See of Rome.”
  • Satis Cognitum §15: the promise is “of the Church as a whole, and not of any certain portions of it. These can indeed be overcome by the assaults of the powers of hell, as in point of fact has befallen some of them.”
  • Berry pp. 56–57: “even the Church of a whole nation may fall away as history abundantly proves.”

Apostolicity of government requires lawful succession with jurisdiction.

  • CE, “Apostolicity” (T. O’Reilly 1907, newadvent.org/cathen/01648b.htm — ! not 01648a): succession “must be both material and formal… the formal adding the element of authority in the transmission of power.” “No one can give a power which he does not possess… no lacuna can be allowed, no new mission can arise.” “jurisdiction is essential to the Apostolicity of mission.” P
  • Van Noort p. 151: the Church “is always ruled by pastors who are the apostles’ legitimate successors.” p. 152 (OCR rebuilt — check a printed copy): such a successor “must… in addition to the power of orders, possess also the power of jurisdiction.” p. 153: “Christ’s Church can never fail to have a successor to Peter.”
  • Berry p. 140: “the power of Orders may be obtained by fraud or conferred against the will of the Church by anyone having valid Orders himself, and therefore does not depend upon legitimate succession… jurisdiction… depends entirely upon legitimate succession.”
  • Vatican I, Pastor Aeternus (Tanner, papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum20.htm): prologue — “it was his will that in his church there should be shepherds and teachers until the end of time”; ch. 2 canon — anathema to one who denies “that blessed Peter should have perpetual successors in the primacy over the whole church.”

But a long vacancy of the Holy See is not excluded.

  • Dorsch, Institutiones Theologiae Fundamentalis vol. II (1928), pp. 196–197 (archive.org — verified, scan partly garbled): “Neque obstat, quod mortuo papa ecclesia per aliquod tempus vel etiam per plures annos capite suo destituta manet; manet enim ejus forma monarchica etiam in isto casu.” “Unde et sedes romana merito dicitur manere defuncto sedente; sedes enim romana habetur essentialiter in iuribus primatus.” (The Roman See is rightly said to remain after its occupant dies, for it consists essentially in the rights of the primacy.)
  • Berry pp. 397–398: “the Apostolic succession cannot fail in the Apostolic See so long as the Church herself continues to exist, for although the see be vacant for many years, the Church always retains the right to elect a legitimate successor.”
  • Fr. Edmund J. O’Reilly SJ, The Relations of the Church to Society (London: John Hodges, 1892, posthumous; archive.org/details/relationschurch00oreiuoft — verified), p. 283, on the Western Schism: “not that an interregnum covering the whole period would have been impossible or inconsistent with the promises of Christ, for this is by no means manifest, but that, as a matter of fact, there was not such an interregnum.” pp. 287–288: “that the true Church should remain between thirty and forty years without a thoroughly ascertained Head… this would not be. Yet it has been; and we have no guarantee that it will not be again… we must not be too ready to pronounce on what God may permit.”

The line the reply must meet. The sources allow a long vacancy of the See. They nowhere envisage a vacancy of every bishop with ordinary jurisdiction: Berry assumes during a vacancy that “the bishops retain power to rule their respective dioceses” (p. 397); Dorsch, that the Church is ruled by power “quae ab uno romano pontifice dimanavit” (p. 197); Satis Cognitum §14, that “the episcopal order necessarily belongs to the essential constitution of the Church.” The problem is not the empty chair; it is the empty sees.

C. The answers §

1. The visible Church is where the three bonds are visibly kept

Visibility is not numbers, buildings or recognition by the world; it is a public society bound by profession of faith, the sacraments and government (A above). A body that publicly fails the first bond is not the visible Church however large it is.

  • Van Noort p. 241: “if public material heretics remained members of the Church, the visibility and unity of Christ’s Church would perish.” → Public profession is constitutive of visibility; a hierarchy that publicly professes what the Church condemned (Sections I–II) cannot be what makes her visible. F
  • Van Noort p. 37 (verified): the Church’s “very existence” is determined by this: “that all profess the doctrine which the apostles taught; that all take part in the same rites which they used to celebrate; and that all obey their rule.” Bond F comes first.
  • CE, “The Church”: failure to recognize the Church is “the blindness of the individual,” not want of visibility. Visibility ≠ being recognized.
  • Thục, Declaratio, Munich, 25 Feb 1982 (research/thuc/en/44-declaratio.md, from the Latin): “What is the appearance of the Catholic Church in our sight, in these days? At Rome there reigns ‘Pope’ John Paul the Second, surrounded by the body of Cardinals and of many Bishops and prelates. Outside Rome the Catholic Church appears flourishing… But in the sight of God, what is the appearance of today’s Church?” — the first sedevacantist bishop’s declaration is an argument about visibility: appearance versus the bonds. It ends: “it behoves me, as a bishop, to do everything so that the Catholic Church at Rome may endure for the eternal salvation of souls” (Latin ut Ecclesia Catholica Romae perduret).
  • The “eclipse” model — John Lane, “Archbishop Lefebvre and the Conciliar Church” (Bellarmine Forums PDF via Wayback): “An eclipse is the placement of one body before another… The obscured body is still exactly what it was, and where it was.” S. D.   • Lefebvre himself moved visibility from the hierarchy to the marks (Écône retreat, 9 Sept 1988; Fideliter 87; wmreview.org/p/lefebvre-visibility-church): “Are they more in the official Church (it is not the visible Church, it is the official Church) or in us…?” … “All this shows that it is we who bear the marks of the visible Church.” → This is the sedevacantist move, made by the objection’s own author.
  • Patristic precedent — the faithful outside the occupied churches (use these, not the Athanasius letter — see D):
    • St. Gregory Nazianzen, Oration 33 §15 (newadvent.org/fathers/310233.htm): “These men have the houses, but we the Dweller in the house; they the Temples, we the God… Is mine a little flock?… I fear not for the little flock; for it is seen at a glance.”
    • St. Basil, Letter 92 §2 (newadvent.org/fathers/3202092.htm): “the better laity shun the churches as schools of impiety; and lift their hands in the deserts with sighs and tears to their Lord in heaven.”

  • Prophetic expectation (supporting, not proof):
    • Luke 18:8 (DRB): “But yet the Son of man, when he cometh, shall he find, think you, faith on earth?”
    • H. E.     • Berry, The Apocalypse of St. John (1921), p. 121: “The Papacy will be attacked by all the powers of hell. In consequence the Church will suffer great trials and afflictions in securing a successor upon the throne of Peter.” p. 123: “the first troubles of those days will be inaugurated within the Church by apostate bishops, priests, and peoples.”
    • Berry, The Church of Christ p. 155 (via Cekada, Unverified page): “There seems to be no reason why a false Church might not become universal, even more universal than the true one, at least for a time.”

2. The papacy is perpetual as an office, not as an unbroken occupancy

  • Dorsch (A): the See “remains” when vacant; the Church stays monarchical “per plures annos” without a head.
  • Berry pp. 397–398 (A): succession “cannot fail in the Apostolic See… although the see be vacant for many years.”
  • Cekada, Traditionalists, Infallibility and the Pope (1995/2006, traditionalmass.org PDF), App. 4–5: “‘Perpetual successors’ means that the office of the Primacy is perpetual… But this papal office can remain vacant for a long time without becoming extinct or changing the nature of the Church.”
  • Fr. Martin Stépanich OFM (traditionalmass.org, “Perpetual Successors”): “‘no pope’ does not mean ‘no papacy.’… Nor did the Church ever specify the length or duration of the vacancy of the Chair of Peter to be allowed.”
  • John Daly / John Lane on O’Reilly (CMRI, 1999, via Wayback): “once it is established in principle that a long interregnum is not incompatible with the promises of Christ, the question of degree — how long — cannot enter into the question.”
  • Historical vacancies and uncertainties (all Catholic Encyclopedia, newadvent.org):
    • 304–308 — after Marcellinus, ~4 years during the Diocletian persecution (“Pope St. Marcellus I”: “nearly two years passed before a new Bishop of Rome was elected” — counted from Maxentius’s accession; the full gap is ~4 years).

The saints themselves were divided.” The same article quotes Didiot: apostolicity “exists objectively in the true pope. What does it matter, in this objective relation, that it is not manifest to all and is not recognized by all till long after?”

3. “Peaceful acceptance” — the objection’s own sources leave room

  • Billot’s proof-text contains, in the same passage, the concession (verified): “Equidem permittere potest Deus ut aliquando vacatio sedis diutius protrahatur.” (God can indeed permit that at times the vacancy of the See be prolonged for a long time.)
  • St. Alphonsus’s next sentence — usually cut (verified, 1767 ed.): “Ma se per qualche tempo non fosse stato veramente accettato universalmente dalla Chiesa, in tal caso per quel tempo sarebbe vacata la Sede Pontificia, come vaca nella morte de’ Pontefici.” (But if for some time he had not been truly accepted universally by the Church, for that time the Pontifical See would have been vacant, as it is at the death of the Popes.) Siscoe’s citation stops before this.
  • Lefebvre’s 1979 test is narrower than “the whole Church”: “unanimous acceptance… by the Cardinals and the Roman clergy.” In the same statement he calls the elections “doubtful at the time: perhaps.”
  • How the thesis should use this: Acceptance makes good defects of election (fraud, irregularity — Alphonsus’s own examples). It cannot make a non-Catholic a member, and divine law bars non-members from the papacy (Part 1: Coronata, Billot, canon 188 §4, Cum ex Apostolatus). And where the acceptance itself is contested — as it has been since Paul VI, from the Ottaviani Intervention onward — Alphonsus’s second sentence applies. Lane cites da Silveira’s admitted “important difficulty… in determining precisely what is the concept of pacific and universal acceptation” (via Lane’s reply to Boulet, novusordowatch.org PDF, Unverified primary).
  • Be honest about the weight. Billot calls universal adhesion an infallible sign. The thesis must argue the conditions were not met; it cannot just deny the principle.

4. How a pope can be elected again

  • O’Reilly p. 287 (verified): “Clearly the Church, when destitute of a Head, or of a certain unquestioned Head, has the right and the power to provide for herself.” p. 187: “a power of providing for itself… in an interregnum in which through some combination of difficulties an election could not be held according to the prescribed form.”
  • Journet, quoting Cajetan (Church of the Word Incarnate pp. 409–411, Downes tr., transcribed at wmreview.org/p/sede-vacante-journet-cajetan): “When the Pope dies the Church is widowed, and, in respect of the visible universal jurisdiction, she is truly acephalous… she possesses the power of the Papacy in potency.” (! Two translations of this passage circulate — Downes “the pulse of life has not left the Church” vs Despósito “heartbeat of life does not cease”; quote one.)
  • Also Billot and Vitoria from the Part 2 conference (Unverified primaries).
  • Novus Ordo Watch (2022): “Although the Church, then, need not actually have a Pope at all times, she must always have one potentially, that is, she must always retain the power to elect one.”
  • Answer to Lefebvre’s “would an angel from heaven provide us with a pope?”: not an angel — the Church, by the devolved power O’Reilly and Cajetan describe. The thesis need not specify the mechanism: Cekada — “an inability at present to determine exactly how another true pope would be chosen in the future does not somehow make Paul VI and his successors into true popes by default.”

5. Jurisdiction and apostolicity of mission — the open question

6. The objection applies to every traditionalist position (tu quoque)

The SSPX and “recognize and resist” cannot use the objection without being caught by it.

D. What the thesis must concede §

  1. The empty-sees problem is real. Every manual that allows a long vacancy assumes bishops with ordinary jurisdiction remain (Berry p. 397; Dorsch p. 197 — “Manet tunc potestas quidem episcoporum in suo ordine; est enim ordinaria”). Van Noort p. 153 says the Church “can never fail to have a successor to Peter,” and p. 154 rejects any Protestant-style “extraordinary mission.” Novus Ordo Watch itself admits “no apparent Catholic bishop left with ordinary jurisdiction.” Name it as the open question; don’t pretend it is solved.
  2. Jerome cuts both ways. The same dialogue argues against separating: §21 “For there is no such thing as a Church without bishops”; §28 “We ought to remain in that Church which was founded by the Apostles and continues to this day.” The bishops of Ariminum were deceived, not formally Arian.
  3. The Western Schism is not a full parallel. There was a true pope at every moment (O’Reilly p. 283); the doubt concerned which person, and “no one wished voluntarily to separate from the head of the Church” (CE). Use it to show the Church survives long uncertainty, not that it has survived a long vacancy.
  4. O’Reilly is hedged. He says only that impossibility “is by no means manifest,” and that in fact there was no such interregnum. Don’t quote him more strongly.
  5. Berry’s Apocalypse and Manning are prophetic readings, not doctrine; Berry’s interregnum follows the martyrdom of a pope in the time of Antichrist. Manning, p. 60, also says “the flock was never scattered: and so it shall be to the end.”
  6. CE “Apostolicity”: in past disputed elections “the matter was always settled by a competent tribunal in the Church, the lawful Pope was proclaimed.” The Church has always ended doubt with a certain pope; the thesis predicts this will happen again, but cannot point to it yet.
  7. Sedevacantists disagree on apostolicity: totalists (Lane, Carmona, CMRI) vs. Cassiciacum (Sanborn, RCI). A thesis cannot claim a settled sedevacantist answer.

E. Recommended thesis position §

  1. Define visibility by the three bonds, from the objectors’ own manuals (Van Noort pp. 12–13, 37; Satis Cognitum §10). Visibility is a public society professing the faith, not numbers, buildings or the world’s recognition (CE “The Church”).
  2. Argue that the conciliar body visibly fails bond F (Sections I–IV), so on Van Noort’s own principle (p. 241) it cannot be what makes the Church visible. The Church remains visible, eclipsed, in the clergy and faithful who keep the faith and the sacraments — the position Lefebvre himself took in 1988.
  3. The papacy is perpetual as an office; a long vacancy is permitted by Dorsch, Berry and Billot, and not shown impossible (O’Reilly). The length is not defined anywhere (Stépanich, Daly).
  4. Peaceful acceptance heals defects of election, not of membership; and Alphonsus’s full text provides for vacancy where acceptance fails.
  5. The power to elect devolves to the Church (O’Reilly, Cajetan/Journet); the mechanism need not be known now (Cekada).
  6. State the jurisdiction question openly as unresolved, set out answers (a)–(d), and press the tu quoque: every traditionalist position lacks ordinary jurisdiction, and only sedevacantism keeps the Church’s teaching and worship spotless. The claim is not “no difficulty,” but “the lesser difficulty.”

This matches the dossier’s general stance: assert the doctrinal claims firmly; present genuinely disputed points as disputed.
